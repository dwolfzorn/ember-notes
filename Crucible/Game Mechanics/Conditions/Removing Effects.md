# Removing Effects

Conditions and other Active Effects usually end when their duration expires. Some, however, can be ended early - and how hard that is to do is set by the effect's **Removal DC**.

## Removal DC

An effect that can be removed bears a numeric **Removal DC**. To end it before its duration runs out, a creature - or an ally acting on its behalf - makes the appropriate skill check, and the effect is removed only if the result *exceeds* the Removal DC. An effect with no Removal DC cannot be shed by these means and must simply be endured until it expires.

When an effect is applied by an action, its Removal DC reflects the power of whoever imposed it: typically 12 plus the relevant ability and skill behind the effect, or 12 plus the originator's level. Effects inflicted by mightier foes are correspondingly harder to escape.

## Effect Properties

An Active Effect may carry one or more **Properties** describing its nature and governing which abilities can act upon it:

- **Magical.** The effect is sustained by magic. All spell-applied effects are Magical, and only anti-magic - a dispel, a cleanse, or the like - can strip a Magical effect, by overcoming its Removal DC. Effects that are not Magical are immune to anti-magic.

## The Escape Action

The most familiar Removal DC is the one checked when breaking free of restraint. A creature suffering the **Restrained** condition may take the **Escape** action, spending 2 Action Points to make an **Athletics** check against the lowest Removal DC among the effects restraining it. Each restraining effect whose Removal DC the result exceeds is ended; if an effect grants other benefits beyond Restrained, only the Restrained condition is stripped from it.
