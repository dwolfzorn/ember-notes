# Party Arcs

> [!info] For the GM
> Each character has one Shard God patron and a personal arc that runs through existing quests, and each arc ends in one of two ways. Quests are listed in rough chapter order. If the party skips a quest, move its beat to whatever comes next. Beats marked **(custom)** don't exist in the vault yet.
>
> **Markers:** A beat marked ◆ is a choice point. Note which way the character leans each time, because those leanings decide which ending they reach.

> [!tip] Reward structure
> Every ending gives the same three kinds of reward:
>
> 1. **Attunement:** 3 [[Attunement]] Points toward the attunement that fits the ending's Ideal, awarded at the climax. You can also give 1 point at each ◆ beat where the character leaned that way, as the [[Attunement]] guide allows.
> 2. **Patron's Gift:** a signature item or blessing. Each one can be used once per Rest unless it says otherwise.
> 3. **Lasting Mark:** a permanent change to the character's place in the world, such as an ally, a title or an open door.

## Silax: the reluctant warrior

**Patron:** [[Thayloc]]
**Ancestry:** Cor'ak

Silax loves peace but keeps being made to fight. Thayloc, a former god of festivals who was consumed by grief after the Evernight, sees himself in Silax and lends them his silver fire, which gets easier to call on and harder to put out with every fight. When Thayloc's zealots come for a harmless dead friend, Silax has to choose: take up Thayloc's war, or remind a broken god who he used to be.

### Beats
1. **[[The Winding Trail]] (Dusktide Festival, [[Helkas]]).** An elderly vendor sells charms of a broken silver arrow and remembers when Thayloc was "the god of good feasts." Silax gets one.
2. **[[Over The Moon]] ([[Lightning in a Bottle]]).** Rorhim Iron-Cask of the [[Fulgurite Blades]] is a Cor'ak who loves fighting and can't understand why Silax doesn't. He shows what people expect Silax to become.
3. ◆ **[[Crumbling Sanctuary]] (undead at Keeper's Crossing).** Silax's strikes leave silver flame on the dead. Does Silax lean into it or hold back?
4. ◆ **[[Smoldering Cinders]] ([[A Conflagration of Lumé]]).** [[Luna Karrowrath]]'s Flameguard serve [[Lumé]], Thayloc's ally, and they invite Silax on their crusade to the [[Barrows]].
5. ◆ **Climax: [[Unfinished Business]] ([[Cost of Living]]).** [[Thayloc Courser]]s try to drag off [[Agraband Swift]], a harmless [[Soulbound]]. Silax decides whether to stand with the Coursers or against them.
6. **Resolution (custom).** Silax meets Thayloc himself, who is hunting in the [[Moiran Bonelands]].

### Ending A: The Hunt
Silax accepts the fire. They side with the Coursers, or at least with Thayloc's mission, and join his war on the undead. Thayloc gains a fierce champion, but Silax gives up some of the peace they loved.

- **Attunement:** Ragen (Directness), 3 points
- **Patron's Gift: Heartbow Splinter.** The arrow charm hardens into silver. When Silax hits an undead creature, it bursts into silver flame and takes an extra 2d8 radiant damage. Undead struck this way can't regain hit points until the start of Silax's next turn.
- **Lasting Mark:** Silax becomes a recognised Courser of Thayloc. The Flameguard and the Coursers become allies. Sionia's followers, and any Soulbound the party meets, become wary of Silax.

### Ending B: The Feast
Silax refuses the war. They protect Agraband, and when they meet Thayloc they talk to him rather than fight beside him, reminding him of the feasts he used to throw. For the first time since the Evernight, Thayloc lays down the Heartbow for a night and holds a festival.

- **Attunement:** Heart (Vitalism), 3 points
- **Patron's Gift: Feastmaker's Arrow.** The charm turns warm gold. As an action, Silax declares a truce. Every creature within 30 ft that can hear Silax must succeed on a Wisdom save (DC 8 + proficiency + Constitution modifier) or be unable to make attacks until the end of Silax's next turn. Undead are immune. During a Rest, the party regains extra hit points equal to Silax's level.
- **Lasting Mark:** Thayloc's cult starts to change and the Coursers spare harmless undead. Thayloc personally owes Silax a favour. Sionia's followers treat Silax as a friend.

## Bones: the healer between battles

**Patron:** [[Raineka]]
**Ancestry:** Fej

Bones is a fire creature devoted to a rain goddess. Years ago, on a Waerd battlefield, Bones saw a hooded woman in a shimmering blue cloak kneeling among the dying, and nobody else remembers her. Following Raineka's trail into Ordain's Healing Houses, Bones finds a shy goddess who heals without condition but despises the undead. Bones has to decide what healing means: tend everyone, or protect the living from what threatens them.

### Beats
1. **[[Ooze Control]] ([[Stone Life]]), happening now.** [[Tauric]] explains that copper-fed oozes can heal injuries. Befriending one gives Bones a living field kit.
2. **[[The Expedition Challenge]] ([[An Auspicious Acquaintance]], [[Troublemaking Duo]]).** A Waerd veteran Bones once patched up wears a Raineka talisman and says, "You saw her too, didn't you?"
3. **[[To Fall and Fall Again]] ([[Magic Incarnate]]).** On a research roll of 20+, a priest of [[Katu]] lets slip that Raineka secretly loves [[Ordain]]. This also starts Mig's arc.
4. ◆ **[[Spreading Sickness]].** Forge workers are falling ill and refugees are close to rioting. Raineka appears hooded at a crowded Healing House. Bones has to decide whether to stay with the patients or go after the people causing the illness.
5. ◆ **Climax: [[Unfinished Business]] ([[Cost of Living]]).** Agraband is dead but still walking. Does Bones treat him like any other patient, or agree with Raineka and the Coursers that the undead must be put to rest?
6. **Resolution (custom).** Raineka takes Bones to the Isle of Storms.

### Ending A: Mercy Without Conditions
Bones treats Agraband like anyone else, and Raineka, who usually keeps her distance, is moved. She takes Bones as her first true confidant in centuries. Bones stays a pacifist to the end.

- **Attunement:** Heart (Vitalism), 3 points
- **Patron's Gift: Raineka's Hood.** A blue cloak that shimmers like falling rain. As a reaction when an ally within 30 ft drops to 0 hit points, Bones can let a gentle rain fall on them. The ally drops to 1 hit point instead and gains temporary hit points equal to Bones's level. While Bones wears the hood, hostile creatures have disadvantage on attacks against Bones on the first turn of combat if Bones hasn't attacked.
- **Lasting Mark:** Bones learns Raineka's hidden spot in Ordain and can find her there. Healing Houses across the Plateau take Bones in. Mig gets a guaranteed meeting with Raineka.

### Ending B: The Shelter in the Storm
Bones decides that protecting the living sometimes means stopping what threatens them, and sides with putting the undead to rest. Bones doesn't become a killer. Bones becomes a protector who controls the field so the dying can be reached. Raineka respects this but keeps her distance.

- **Attunement:** Akon (Discipline), 3 points
- **Patron's Gift: Stormwall Charm.** A talisman like those carried by Waerd warriors. As an action, Bones raises a 15-ft-radius ring of driving rain centred on Bones for 1 minute. Bones and allies inside have half cover against ranged attacks and advantage on death saves. Undead that start their turn inside take 2d6 radiant damage.
- **Lasting Mark:** The Waerd factions honour Bones as a battlefield healer and give safe passage in the [[Lowlands]]. Bones gains a warm standing with Thayloc's Coursers, which is useful if Silax took Ending A.

## Olli: the sentinel who forgot

**Patron:** [[Steelsong]]
**Also involved:** [[Mioroth]], [[Vhismara]]
**Ancestry:** Hulg'run

Olli is a Shent sentinel, built before the Shent fell to watch for the Abyss, and woken because it is stirring again. Olli doesn't remember this and thinks the pull toward "the Shroud" is curiosity. Shent ruins recognise him, Mioroth stays silent about him, and an Outer God offers him the thing he wants most, which is understanding. Olli has to choose between the purpose he was built for and the void that purpose was meant to watch.

> [!warning] Player knowledge
> The vault warns that player characters normally don't know the words Abyss, Outer Gods or Abyssal Shear. Olli should feel drawn to "the Shroud" without knowing why, and the real names come out during play.

### Beats
1. **[[Ooze Control]] ([[Stone Life#Investigating the Island|Investigating the Island]], [[Yakoshta Mine]]), happening now.** When Olli gets close, the Shent runes flare. [[Tauric]], a fellow Hulg'run, notices that Olli's spirit core feels older than any he's met.
2. **[[Over The Moon]] ([[Lunar Awakenings]]).** [[Mioroth]] looks at Olli a beat too long and won't say why. In the [[Writhing Grave]], the [[Writhing Whisperer]] aims its offers of power at Olli.
3. **[[Signal of Intent]] ([[Multifaceted]]).** In the [[Kaleidoscope Caverns]], the Shent died carving unfinished mind-magic crystals. Olli's spirit core is a finished one. When he touches the destroyed machine, the [[Abyssal Echo]]es go for him first.
4. ◆ **[[Glitter in the Dark]].** [[Hew]] is another construct who woke up. [[Vartholomew Chess]] confirms that no modern hands built Olli. Does Olli defend awakened constructs or keep his distance?
5. **[[Ancient Paths]] ([[Impossible Skin]]).** When Olli leaves an offering at the shrine to Steelsong, the ash rearranges into a Shent rune.
6. ◆ **[[A Brush With Death]] ([[Where Evil Lurks]]).** [[The Bleak Archive]] recognises Olli. Does he take or study the [[Finger of Nethehepticas]], or seal it away? Mioroth gives him one true piece of his past.
7. ◆ **Climax: [[To Fall and Fall Again]] ([[Snarled Promises]], [[Primordial Bastion]]).** The Snarling Voice offers Olli understanding. Afterwards, [[Spectra]] reveals what Olli was built to watch for.
8. **Resolution (custom).** An envoy from Steelsong's Krackos Enclave comes for Olli.

### Ending A: The Sentinel Restored
Olli turns down the Voice and takes back his purpose. With Mioroth's memories and Steelsong's forge he gets the rest of his past back and chooses to keep watching, this time because he wants to. Steelsong wants to build more sentinels using him as the template, and Olli decides whether to allow it.

- **Attunement:** Signara (Curiosity), 3 points
- **Patron's Gift: Sentinel Core.** Steelsong finishes the crystal at Olli's centre. Olli always knows when Abyssal energy or an Abyssal creature is within 120 ft. Once per Rest, as a reaction when Olli or an ally within 30 ft fails a save against a charm, fear or psychic effect, Olli can make them reroll it and use the new result.
- **Lasting Mark:** Olli is welcome in the Krackos Enclave and in Shent ruins, which open to him. Mioroth treats Olli as an heir.

### Ending B: Eyes in the Dark
Olli listens to the Voice. He doesn't serve it. He learns from it and gets the understanding he wanted, at a price. He now sees the Abyss more clearly than any mortal alive, and it sees him back. Steelsong is afraid of what he could become, and so is Olli.

- **Attunement:** Abyss (Nihilism), 3 points. If the party gained a [[Beast Mark]], Olli carries the deepest one.
- **Patron's Gift: Void-Touched Core.** The crystal turns black with a green glow in its depths. Once per Rest, as an action, Olli opens a 10-ft-radius rift of void within 60 ft. Creatures inside must make a Wisdom save or take 3d8 psychic damage and be frightened until the end of their next turn. Olli can also understand any Abyssal speech or script.
- **Lasting Mark:** Olli can ask the Snarling Voice one question per chapter and always gets a true answer, but each question costs him a Beast Mark. Steelsong's envoy is now watching him rather than recruiting him. [[Spectra]] takes a personal, wary interest in Olli.

## Ffion: are plants people?

**Patron:** [[Aythorn]]
**Ancestry:** Thornling

Ffion is fashionable and wants to know whether other plants can think. Aythorn, the Plateau's beloved trickster god, claims to be an ascended Thornling but doesn't look or act like one. The truth is that Aythorn was once an ordinary plant that woke up. Following that question takes Ffion from crawling hedges to a fungus that speaks to a creeping corruption of obsidian vines, and Ffion has to decide what to do with the answer: protect every waking plant, or protect the garden from the ones that would consume it.

### Beats
1. ◆ **[[Thorny Predicaments]] ([[Planting a Seed]]).** [[Edivel Sprout]]'s spell has animated a hedge. Does Ffion talk to it or clear it off the road?
2. **[[Natural Wonders]], [[Bridging the Gap]].** [[Brevin]] is a seed-grown town. Is the town itself alive?
3. **[[Aythorn's Blessings]].** Aythorn deflects Ffion's questions and gives Ffion a flower crown that turns toward Ffion every morning.
4. ◆ **[[Strange Thornlings]] (Standalone Event, Pathways).** The **Sporix** is a semi-sentient fungus that names itself. Does Ffion try to communicate with it or destroy it?
5. **[[Disgraced House]] ([[The Marlstone Gala]]).** Ffion's chance to dress up. Aythorn admits they were "a bit like those flowers once."
6. **[[Local Color]].** The murals take aim at House Salva's grip on the fashion industry. This is a side beat for Ffion's interest in fashion.
7. ◆ **Climax: [[Disturbed Earth]] (Chapter 4).** The obsidian vines spreading from [[Slaith]]'s corruption are close to waking. Does Ffion try to save them or cut them out?

### Ending A: The Voice of the Green
Ffion tries to save the vines and frees some of them from the corruption. Aythorn finally confesses the truth about where they came from and recognises Ffion as a fellow speaker for the green. Ffion becomes the first mortal advocate for plants that are waking up.

- **Attunement:** Orbis (Spontaneity), 3 points
- **Patron's Gift: Aythorn's Living Couture.** An outfit grown from living plants that blooms to match any style. Ffion can speak with plants at will and gets advantage on social checks at formal events. Once per Rest, Ffion can ask the outfit to animate nearby plants: a 20-ft square becomes difficult terrain, and one creature in it must make a Strength save or be restrained until the end of its next turn.
- **Lasting Mark:** Brevin and the Agrimage Circle treat Ffion as an authority on plant awareness. Aythorn calls Ffion "Boba," a Thornling word for close family. Some freed vines in the Redrak Fields remember Ffion.

### Ending B: The Gardener's Shears
Ffion decides that awareness doesn't give something the right to consume others, which the Sporix showed, and cuts out the corruption. Aythorn is sad and still tells the truth, but warns Ffion that "some of what you prune might have been like me."

- **Attunement:** Cora (Pragmatism), 3 points
- **Patron's Gift: Thornsilk Shears.** A pair of fashionable silver shears that can be used as a weapon. Attacks with them deal an extra 1d8 damage to plant creatures and creatures infected by parasites. Once per Rest, Ffion can cut a disease, curse or parasitic infection out of a willing creature, ending one such effect.
- **Lasting Mark:** The Agrimage Circle and House Lillifeld honour Ffion for saving the harvest. Ffion is known as a protector of Thornlings, and the Sporix and anything like it will recognise Ffion as an enemy.

## Mig: four storms

**Patrons:** The Family of Storms: [[Katu]] (storms), [[Vekalla]] (wind), [[Raineka]] (rain) and [[Sokali]] (snow)
**Ancestry:** Altyra

Mig wants elemental power, and no single god will give it. Learning that the Family of Storms lives on a hidden floating island, Mig sets out to win over each member: Vekalla through negotiation, Raineka through compassion, Katu through courage and finally Sokali through cold, which is an Altyra's greatest weakness. Mig ends by choosing between becoming the family's heir after finishing every trial, or taking the storm without their blessing.

### Beats
1. **[[Over The Moon]] ([[Shent Moon Temple]]).** Hostile water elementals ([[Hydroxol]], [[Water Mote]]) give Mig a first, uncontrolled spark of elemental power.
2. **[[To Fall and Fall Again]] ([[Magic Incarnate]]).** A research roll of 18+ turns up a priest of Katu who reveals the Isle of Storms, a floating island anchored off the south-east [[Arctus Plateau]]. On 20+ he also gives away Raineka's secret, which is Bones's lead too.
3. ◆ **Vekalla's trial: [[Diplomatic Impunity]] ([[Parcels & Pirates]]).** The negotiation with [[Gastern Faviyos]] and the Wind Raiders. Talking your way through counts for more than winning the fight.
4. ◆ **Raineka's trial: [[Spreading Sickness]].** This runs alongside Bones's arc. Will Mig put their own ambition on hold to help someone?
5. ◆ **Katu's trial (custom).** A storm at sea, perhaps on the voyage in Diplomatic Impunity, that Mig has to face rather than shelter from.
6. ◆ **Climax: Sokali's trial (custom).** A winter crossing where Mig's body starts to crack, and Mig either endures or takes a shortcut.
7. **Resolution (custom).** Mig reaches the Isle of Storms.

### Ending A: Heir of the Storms
Mig finishes all four trials, including Sokali's cold, and is welcomed to the Isle of Storms as a child of the family. The power Mig gains is shared, and the family will expect Mig's help in return.

- **Attunement:** Mayis (Patience), 3 points
- **Patron's Gift: Fourfold Sigil.** A storm-sigil appears among the Luxarum glyphs on Mig's face. Mig ignores the slowing and brittleness that cold causes in Altyra, thanks to Sokali. Once per Rest, Mig can call on one family member: Katu (a 30-ft line of lightning that deals 4d8 damage, Dexterity save for half), Vekalla (Mig and allies within 30 ft gain 20 ft of speed and can't be knocked prone for 1 minute), Raineka (up to three allies regain 2d8 hit points) or Sokali (a 15-ft cone of frost that deals 3d8 cold damage, and targets that fail a Constitution save are restrained).
- **Lasting Mark:** The family adopts Mig, and the Isle of Storms is open to the party. Sailors and Vekalla's traders give Mig safe passage. Katu will answer one summons for help in a crisis.

### Ending B: The Stolen Storm
Mig takes a shortcut, maybe skipping Sokali's trial or taking power from Katu's storm without permission. Mig gets the power, alone and on Mig's own terms. Katu is impressed, Vekalla is amused, and Raineka and Sokali turn away.

- **Attunement:** Aura (Individualism), 3 points
- **Patron's Gift: Captured Tempest.** Mig's metal blood carries a trapped storm, and Mig's Altyran transformation crackles with it. Once per Rest, while transformed, Mig can release it as a 20-ft-radius burst centred on Mig. Each creature in it must make a Dexterity save, taking 6d6 lightning damage and being pushed 15 ft on a failure, or half damage and no push on a success. Mig stays vulnerable to cold, and whenever Mig takes cold damage the storm hurts Mig too, dealing 1d6 extra damage.
- **Lasting Mark:** Katu respects Mig as a rival and will test them again. Sokali hunts Mig through the winters as a game of predator and prey. Raineka will no longer meet Mig, which strains Mig's relationship with Bones if Bones took Ending A.

## Shared threads

- **The Isle of Storms** is where several arcs meet. Mig gets their final trials there, Raineka brings Bones, and Ffion can go along with Aythorn, who is a friend of the family.
- **The Ordain secret.** The priest's slip in [[Magic Incarnate]] opens both Bones's and Mig's arcs in a single scene.
- **Made for war.** Silax was forced to fight, and Olli was built for a war he doesn't remember. If Steelsong wants to build more sentinels, Silax is the party member most likely to object.
- **[[Cost of Living]] is a joint climax.** Silax decides about the Coursers and Bones decides about Agraband in the same scene, and the two choices can pull against each other.
- **Endings that connect:**
  - If Silax takes The Hunt and Bones takes The Shelter, the Coursers become the party's allies.
  - If Silax takes The Feast and Bones takes Mercy, Thayloc and Raineka are brought together.
  - If Mig takes The Stolen Storm and Bones takes Mercy, the two are pulled apart.
  - If Olli takes Eyes in the Dark, Steelsong may ask Silax to keep an eye on Olli.
- **Hidden truths.** Mioroth knows what Olli is, and Aythorn knows what Ffion is looking for. Both are withholding it. That parallel can be pointed out in play.
