# Party Arcs

> [!info] For the GM
> Each character has one Shard God patron and a personal arc that runs through existing quests, and each arc ends in one of two ways. Quests are listed in rough chapter order. If the party skips a quest, move its beat to whatever comes next. Beats marked **(custom)** don't exist in the vault yet.
>
> **Markers:** A beat marked ◆ is a choice point. Note which way the character leans each time, because those leanings decide which ending they reach. A beat marked ↺ is a flashback scene.

> [!tip] Reward structure
> Every ending gives the same three kinds of reward:
>
> 1. **Attunement:** 3 [[Attunement]] Points toward the attunement that fits the ending's Ideal, awarded at the climax. You can also give 1 point at each ◆ beat where the character leaned that way, as the [[Attunement]] guide allows.
> 2. **Patron's Gift:** a signature item or blessing. Each one can be used once per Rest unless it says otherwise.
> 3. **Lasting Mark:** a permanent change to the character's place in the world, such as an ally, a title or an open door.

## Silax: the one who strikes first

**Patron:** [[Thayloc]]
**Ancestry:** Cor'ak

Silax's clan prized violence above everything and taught that you always strike first. It never came naturally to him. Travelling with Bones, who refuses to fight, Silax has become the one who strikes so that Bones never has to, and he has proved good at it. Thayloc's edict is "Swift is my arrow for rotting hearts." He was once a god of festivals, and after failing to act in time during the Evernight he became a god who never waits. He sees in Silax the champion his clan raised him to be. Silax has to decide whose way he will follow: his clan's and Thayloc's, or Bones's.

### Beats
1. **[[The Winding Trail]] (Dusktide Festival, [[Helkas]]).** An elderly vendor sells charms of a broken silver arrow and remembers when Thayloc was "the god of good feasts." Silax gets one. When the Otherhood of Fortune attacks the festival, Silax's clan instincts take over before he has time to think.
2. **[[Over The Moon]] ([[Lightning in a Bottle]]).** Rorhim Iron-Cask of the [[Fulgurite Blades]] is a Cor'ak who fights for the joy of it. He teases Silax for hesitating "like someone raised in a city," which is exactly what Silax's clan used to say.
3. ◆ **[[Crumbling Sanctuary]] ([[Harrowed Crossing]]).** When the [[Sodden Corpse]]s rise from the river, Silax's strikes leave silver flame on them. Does he strike before Bones can reach the wounded, or hold the line and let Bones work?
4. ◆ **[[Smoldering Cinders]] ([[A Conflagration of Lumé]]).** [[Luna Karrowrath]]'s Flameguard serve [[Lumé]], Thayloc's closest ally. They admire Silax's instincts and sneer at Bones, whose reputation reaches them from the Lumek front (see Bones's arc). They invite Silax on their crusade to the [[Barrows]]. Does he go?
5. ◆ **Climax: [[Unfinished Business]] ([[Cost of Living]]).** [[Thayloc Courser]]s ambush the party to seize [[Agraband Swift]], a harmless [[Soulbound]]. They strike first, the way Silax was taught to. Silax has to choose between standing with the Coursers and standing with Bones and Agraband. This is the same moment as Bones's climax.
6. ◆ **Echo: [[Proving Your Metal]] (Chapter 4 Standalone Event, [[Redrak Fields]]).** A [[Burnished Hand]] patrol stops the party and suspects them. The event can be won with persuasion or with martial contests. This is the clan's test in miniature: does Silax talk the patrol down, or does he prove himself the way his clan would? [[Carver Pela Melir]], who sees passing on martial tradition as her duty, will read Silax as either a warrior who's lost his way or one who has found a better one.
7. **Resolution (custom): [[Mistwalk]] and [[Khar'koridax]] ([[Barrows]]).** Mistwalk is a settlement where several faiths have banded together against the undead, and it's the obvious place for the Flameguard's crusade to gather. Thayloc arrives to clear out Khar'koridax, a Varún tomb whose dead serve [[Nethehepticas]]. Silax meets him at the tomb's entrance.

### Ending A: The Swift Arrow
Silax accepts what his clan taught him and gives it a purpose by joining Thayloc's war on the undead. He goes into Khar'koridax at Thayloc's side and stops apologising for striking first. Bones's way remains something Silax protects without sharing.

- **Attunement:** Ragen (Directness), 3 points
- **Patron's Gift: Heartbow Splinter.** The arrow charm hardens into silver. Silax has advantage on initiative rolls. When Silax hits an undead creature, it bursts into silver flame and takes an extra 2d8 radiant damage, and it can't regain hit points until the start of Silax's next turn.
- **Lasting Mark:** Silax becomes a recognised Courser of Thayloc. The Flameguard, the Coursers and the defenders of Mistwalk become allies. Sionia's followers, and any Soulbound the party meets, become wary of Silax. Silax's clan would be proud of him, which may not feel as good as he expected.

### Ending B: The Open Hand
Silax refuses to strike first. He protects Agraband, and when he meets Thayloc he talks to him rather than fighting beside him, offering the god the lesson Bones taught him. For the first time since the Evernight, Thayloc lays down the Heartbow for a night and holds a festival in Mistwalk.

- **Attunement:** Heart (Vitalism), 3 points
- **Patron's Gift: Feastmaker's Arrow.** The charm turns warm gold. As an action, Silax declares a truce. Every creature within 30 ft that can hear Silax must succeed on a Wisdom save (DC 8 + proficiency + Constitution modifier) or be unable to make attacks until the end of Silax's next turn. Undead are immune. **If a creature attacks Silax or Bones during the truce, Silax can make one attack against it as a reaction.** During a Rest, the party regains extra hit points equal to Silax's level.
- **Lasting Mark:** Thayloc's cult starts to change and the Coursers spare harmless undead. Thayloc personally owes Silax a favour. Mistwalk remembers the feast. Silax has broken with his clan, which a future custom scene could follow up.

## Bones: the coward medic

**Patron:** [[Raineka]]
**Ancestry:** Fej
**Told through:** present-day beats and ↺ flashbacks

Bones grew up on the Lumek front lines, where the Fej have fought the undead [[Moiran]] for generations under [[Lumé]]. He refused to carry a blade. The soldiers called him a coward and still lined up for his stitches. His commander, **Commander Kesh Durannor** (rename freely), was the one person who respected that choice. In the end Kesh followed Bones's example by refusing to strike first, and died for it. Fej burn to ash when they die, and Bones carries Kesh's ashes. The Lumek forge the ashes of their dead into holy weapons of [[Ambral Bronze]], and Bones has refused to hand Kesh over. As Kesh lay dying, a hooded woman in a cloak that shimmered like rain knelt beside him and did not save him. Bones has followed Raineka ever since, wanting to know why, and to find out whether Kesh's mercy was right.

### Beats
1. ↺ **[[Ooze Control]] ([[Stone Life]]), happening now.** [[Tauric]] explains that copper-fed oozes can heal injuries. *Flashback:* Bones's first muster as a young Fej, refusing the blade he's handed. Kesh hands him a medic's satchel instead and says, "Then you'd better be fast."
2. ↺ **[[Crumbling Sanctuary]] ([[The Blockaded Bridge]]).** The party meets [[Luna Karrowrath]]'s company of [[Lumek]] warriors at the blockade. One of them recognises Bones and says the old slur, "ash-hand." Luna knows Kesh's ashes were never returned for forging and demands to know why. *Flashback:* the field hospital, where the same soldiers who mocked Bones by day begged for him at night.
3. ↺ **[[Smoldering Cinders]] ([[A Conflagration of Lumé]]).** The Flameguard are split. Some scorn Bones and others show him the scars he stitched. The bonfire ceremony that blesses their crusade is where the ashes question comes back: Kesh "should be fighting with us." *Flashback:* Kesh asks Bones, privately, how he can stand never fighting back. Bones's answer stays with Kesh.
4. **[[To Fall and Fall Again]] ([[Magic Incarnate]]).** On a research roll of 20+, a priest of [[Katu]] lets slip that Raineka secretly loves [[Ordain]] and has a favourite spot there. Bones can find her.
5. ◆ **[[Spreading Sickness]].** Forge workers are falling ill and refugees are close to rioting. Raineka appears hooded at a crowded Healing House. Bones confronts her about Kesh, and she doesn't answer yet. Does Bones stay with the patients or go after the people causing the illness?
6. ◆↺ **Climax: [[Unfinished Business]] ([[Cost of Living]]).** The Coursers' ambush plays out almost exactly like the day Kesh died. *Flashback:* Kesh's company faces a group of Moiran who appear to be surrendering, or living refugees mixed with undead. Kesh orders his soldiers to hold and not strike first. The enemy strikes, and Kesh falls shielding Bones. Back in the present, does Bones treat Agraband like any other patient, or accept that some threats have to be stopped? This is the same moment as Silax's climax.
7. **Echo: [[The Flood and the Flame]] ([[Burning Hearts]], Chapter 4).** The party has to choose between killing [[Kess the Firemage]] and attempting a ritual that might save her. Whatever Bones decided at his climax, the party will look to him here.
8. **Resolution (custom).** Raineka finally tells Bones what happened when Kesh died. Kesh asked her to tend the others first, and she honoured his wish. Then she takes Bones to the Isle of Storms. If the party has met [[Sorak the Stormheart]] at [[Katino Shrine]], Bones can tell him that Katu's family hasn't abandoned everyone.

### Ending A: Kesh's Way
Bones treats Agraband like anyone else and decides that Kesh's mercy was right even though it killed him. Bones has Kesh's ashes forged into Ambral Bronze, but as a protective charm of the kind the Lumek wear against undeath and disease, not as a weapon. Raineka is moved and takes Bones as her first true confidant in centuries. Bones stays a pacifist to the end.

- **Attunement:** Heart (Vitalism), 3 points
- **Patron's Gift: Raineka's Hood.** A blue cloak that shimmers like falling rain, fastened with an Ambral Bronze clasp made from Kesh's ashes. As a reaction when an ally within 30 ft drops to 0 hit points, Bones can let a gentle rain fall on them. The ally drops to 1 hit point instead and gains temporary hit points equal to Bones's level. While Bones wears the hood, hostile creatures have disadvantage on attacks against Bones on the first turn of combat if Bones hasn't attacked.
- **Lasting Mark:** Bones learns Raineka's hidden spot in Ordain and can find her there. Among the Lumek, "ash-hand" stops being an insult, and the clasp is the first Ambral Bronze in memory made for healing. Luna Karrowrath's company owes Bones a debt. Healing Houses across the Plateau take him in.

### Ending B: Kesh's Shield
Bones decides that Kesh died because nobody was protecting the person showing mercy. Bones still doesn't become a killer, but he stops letting the people he heals stand unprotected. He has Kesh's ashes forged into a shield-talisman so that Kesh can still protect people. Raineka respects the decision and keeps her distance.

- **Attunement:** Akon (Discipline), 3 points
- **Patron's Gift: Stormwall Charm.** An Ambral Bronze talisman forged from Kesh's ashes. As an action, Bones raises a 15-ft-radius ring of driving rain centred on him for 1 minute. Bones and allies inside have half cover against ranged attacks and advantage on death saves. Undead that start their turn inside take 2d6 radiant damage.
- **Lasting Mark:** The Lumek honour Bones as a battlefield healer and give him a place at Lumé's front if he ever wants it. Thayloc's Coursers also respect him.

## Olli: the sentinel who forgot

**Patron:** [[Sigil]]
**Also involved:** [[Mioroth]], [[Vhismara]], [[Steelsong]] (through Mig)
**Ancestry:** Hulg'run

Olli is a Shent sentinel, built before the Shent fell to watch for the Abyss, and woken because it is stirring again. Olli doesn't remember this and thinks his pull toward "the Shroud" is curiosity. Sigil has catalogued almost everything in the world, but his entry on Shent sentinels has its pages missing, and he wants Olli's story preserved before it's lost again. Shent ruins recognise Olli, Mioroth stays silent about him, and an Outer God offers him the thing he wants most, which is understanding. Olli has to choose between the purpose he was built for and the void that purpose was meant to watch.

> [!warning] Player knowledge
> The vault warns that player characters normally don't know the words Abyss, Outer Gods or Abyssal Shear. Olli should feel drawn to "the Shroud" without knowing why, and the real names come out during play.

### Beats
1. **[[Ooze Control]] ([[Stone Life#Investigating the Island|Investigating the Island]], [[Yakoshta Mine]]), happening now.** When Olli gets close, the Shent runes flare. [[Tauric]], a fellow Hulg'run, notices that Olli's spirit core feels older than any he's met.
2. **[[Over The Moon]] ([[Lunar Awakenings]]).** [[Mioroth]] looks at Olli a beat too long and won't say why. In the [[Writhing Grave]], the [[Writhing Whisperer]] aims its offers of power at Olli.
3. **[[Signal of Intent]] ([[Multifaceted]]).** In the [[Kaleidoscope Caverns]], the Shent died carving unfinished mind-magic crystals. Olli's spirit core is a finished one. When he touches the destroyed machine, the [[Abyssal Echo]]es go for him first. Mig wants to take the machine apart.
4. **[[An Old Friend]] ([[A Friendly Reunion]], [[Oldcraft Lodge]]).** Sigil's living beard pulls out a scroll on "Shent Sentinels" with every page after the first one torn out. Sigil asks Olli to bring back what he learns.
5. ◆ **[[Glitter in the Dark]] ([[Junkyard Cogs]]).** [[Hew]] is another construct who woke up, and the Silver Beam Consortium is converting Chessmen into servitors. [[Vartholomew Chess]] confirms that no modern hands built Olli. Does Olli defend awakened constructs or keep his distance?
6. **[[Ancient Paths]] ([[Impossible Skin]]).** The shrine there honours both Sigil and Steelsong. When Olli leaves an offering, the ash rearranges into a Shent rune.
7. ◆ **[[A Brush With Death]] ([[Where Evil Lurks]]).** [[The Bleak Archive]] recognises Olli. Does he take or study the [[Finger of Nethehepticas]], or seal it away? Mioroth gives him one true piece of his past.
8. ◆ **Climax: [[To Fall and Fall Again]] ([[Snarled Promises]], [[Primordial Bastion]]).** The Snarling Voice offers Olli understanding. Afterwards, [[Spectra]] reveals what Olli was built to watch for.
9. **Echo: [[Ruby Grove]] ([[Where Shadows Lie]], [[Kadra Zann]], Chapter 4).** Kadra Zann is a Shent temple to Zor-Thal, the Builder, a god of the [[Sentina]] pantheon. On the [[Atrium Floor]], six globe-topped pillars were once a **Shent cosmological scrying device**, a machine built to watch the sky as Olli was built to watch the void. The Doomsayers have turned it into a tool for [[Primordis]]. Olli recognises the device the way you'd recognise a sibling, and the statue of Zor-Thal may answer who designed sentinels. Characters with Knowledge: Shent automatically succeed on the statue check.
10. **Echo: [[The Flood and the Flame]] (Chapter 4).** [[Fiyara]] is a fire sovereign corrupted by the Abyss, and her Voidfire is Abyssal fire. Olli's core aches near every [[Voidfire Cinder]]. If Olli took Ending B, the Voidfire calls to him by name.
11. **Resolution (custom).** Olli brings his story back to Oldcraft Lodge.

### Ending A: The Sentinel Restored
Olli turns down the Voice and takes back his purpose. Sigil's archive and Mioroth's memories restore his past. At Steelsong's forge, with Mig helping, the crystal at his centre is finished. Olli chooses to keep watching, this time because he wants to.

- **Attunement:** Signara (Curiosity), 3 points
- **Patron's Gift: Sentinel Core.** Olli always knows when Abyssal energy or an Abyssal creature is within 120 ft. Once per Rest, as a reaction when Olli or an ally within 30 ft fails a save against a charm, fear or psychic effect, Olli can make them reroll it and use the new result.
- **Lasting Mark:** Sigil writes Olli's story into the archive and gives him a key to Oldcraft Lodge. Shent ruins open to him. Mioroth treats Olli as an heir. If the Kadra Zann scrying device survives, Olli can learn to use it.

### Ending B: Eyes in the Dark
Olli listens to the Voice. He doesn't serve it. He learns from it and gets the understanding he wanted, at a price. He now sees the Abyss more clearly than any mortal alive, and it sees him back. Sigil seals Olli's entry in the archive, and doesn't say whether that's to protect Olli or to protect everyone else from him.

- **Attunement:** Abyss (Nihilism), 3 points. If the party gained a [[Beast Mark]], Olli carries the deepest one.
- **Patron's Gift: Void-Touched Core.** The crystal turns black with a green glow in its depths. Once per Rest, as an action, Olli opens a 10-ft-radius rift of void within 60 ft. Creatures inside must make a Wisdom save or take 3d8 psychic damage and be frightened until the end of their next turn. Olli can also understand any Abyssal speech or script.
- **Lasting Mark:** Olli can ask the Snarling Voice one question per chapter and always gets a true answer, but each question costs him a Beast Mark. [[Spectra]] takes a personal, wary interest in Olli. Steelsong asks Mig to keep an eye on him.

## Ffion: are plants people?

**Patron:** [[Aythorn]]
**Ancestry:** Thornling

Ffion is fashionable and wants to know whether other plants can think. Aythorn, the Plateau's beloved trickster god, claims to be an ascended Thornling but doesn't look or act like one. The truth is that Aythorn was once an ordinary plant that woke up. Following that question takes Ffion from crawling hedges to a fungus that speaks, then to a forest that fights back and a living tree from another realm, and finally to a creeping corruption of obsidian vines. Ffion has to decide what to do with the answer: protect every waking plant, or protect the garden from the ones that would consume it.

### Beats
1. ◆ **[[Thorny Predicaments]] ([[Planting a Seed]]).** [[Edivel Sprout]]'s spell has animated a hedge. Does Ffion talk to it or clear it off the road?
2. **[[Natural Wonders]], [[Bridging the Gap]].** [[Brevin]] is a seed-grown town. Is the town itself alive?
3. **[[Aythorn's Blessings]].** Aythorn deflects Ffion's questions and gives Ffion a flower crown that turns toward Ffion every morning.
4. ◆ **[[Strange Thornlings]] (Standalone Event, Pathways).** The **Sporix** is a semi-sentient fungus that names itself. Does Ffion try to communicate with it or destroy it?
5. **[[Disgraced House]] ([[The Marlstone Gala]]).** Ffion's chance to dress up. Aythorn admits they were "a bit like those flowers once."
6. **[[Local Color]].** The murals take aim at House Salva's grip on the fashion industry. This is a side beat for Ffion's interest in fashion.
7. **[[Ruby Grove]] ([[Just a Taste]], Chapter 4).** The Most August Society of Aythorn is a group of revellers in Ordain. One of them, Barka, is a Thornling worshipper of Aythorn. They're Ffion's kind of people: fashionable, chaotic and devoted. They introduce Ruby, the jam harvested from Bloodwoods galls that's become a fashionable drug in Ordain, and invite the party to [[Talei]].
8. ◆ **[[Ruby Grove]] ([[Bloodwoods]], [[Local Experts]], [[Where Shadows Lie]]).** This is the strongest evidence yet:
   - The black aspens of the Bloodwoods writhe to throw out intruders ([[Traversing the Bloodwoods]]).
   - The [[Arbore Sanctorus]] is a living tree from [[Primordis]], and its rooms are carved from its own body. It was planted by [[Nimaelle]], a Casia, to heal a scar left by the Abyssal Shear.
   - At [[Kadra Zann]], the sap of the [[Shadebranch]] turns people into fey [[Grayling]]. Here a plant changes people, the reverse of Ffion's question.

   Ffion can ask Nimaelle directly whether the tree minds. Does Ffion side with the living forest, or with the people it's swallowing?
9. ◆ **Climax: [[Disturbed Earth]] (Chapter 4).** The obsidian vines spreading from [[Slaith]]'s corruption are close to waking. Does Ffion try to save them or cut them out?

### Ending A: The Voice of the Green
Ffion tries to save the vines and frees some of them from the corruption. Aythorn finally confesses the truth about where they came from and recognises Ffion as a fellow speaker for the green. Ffion becomes the first mortal advocate for plants that are waking up.

- **Attunement:** Orbis (Spontaneity), 3 points
- **Patron's Gift: Aythorn's Living Couture.** An outfit grown from living plants that blooms to match any style. Ffion can speak with plants at will and gets advantage on social checks at formal events. Once per Rest, Ffion can ask the outfit to animate nearby plants: a 20-ft square becomes difficult terrain, and one creature in it must make a Strength save or be restrained until the end of its next turn.
- **Lasting Mark:** Brevin and the Agrimage Circle treat Ffion as an authority on plant awareness. The Most August Society of Aythorn adopts Ffion as a guest of honour. Aythorn calls Ffion "Boba," a Thornling word for close family. Nimaelle and the Sanguinaries welcome Ffion deeper into the Arbore Sanctorus than most visitors get.

### Ending B: The Gardener's Shears
Ffion decides that awareness doesn't give something the right to consume others, which the Sporix and the Shadebranch both showed, and cuts out the corruption. Aythorn is sad and still tells the truth, but warns Ffion that "some of what you prune might have been like me."

- **Attunement:** Cora (Pragmatism), 3 points
- **Patron's Gift: Thornsilk Shears.** A pair of fashionable silver shears that can be used as a weapon. Attacks with them deal an extra 1d8 damage to plant creatures and creatures infected by parasites. Once per Rest, Ffion can cut a disease, curse or parasitic infection out of a willing creature, ending one such effect. This includes a Grayling transformation that hasn't finished.
- **Lasting Mark:** The Agrimage Circle and [[House Lilifeld]] honour Ffion for saving the harvest. Talei's ruby collectors trust Ffion. The Sporix, the Silent Pack and anything like them will recognise Ffion as an enemy.

## Mig: the gunsmith's question

**Patron:** [[Steelsong]]
**Rival influence:** [[Lorgi]]
**Ancestry:** Altyra

Mig is a fire mage and gunslinger who understands the world by taking it apart to see how it works. Her own liquid-metal body is one more mechanism she means to figure out. Steelsong, the goddess of craftsmanship, invention and fire, builds by hand at the Krackos Enclave forge to protect her people. Lorgi, the Wild God of fire and technology, leads the Tayan Empire's Mechanica, and his Aethyra breakthroughs are faster and more powerful but serve an empire. Firearms are Tayan technology, so Mig's craft already leads toward Lorgi. In Chapter 4 Mig meets her own reflection in **Kess the Firemage**, a young fire mage who wanted only fire and was taken by [[Fiyara]], Lorgi's sister, who was corrupted by the Abyss. Mig has to decide what her fire, and her inventions, are for.

> [!note] Lorgi is a Wild God
> Wild Gods can't hear prayers from far away. Mig has to deal with Lorgi in person or through Tayan agents. That makes him a rival influence rather than a patron.

### Beats
1. **[[Ooze Control]] ([[Stone Life]]), happening now.** Feeding metal dust to oozes changes them: iron makes them magnetic and silver makes them electric. Mig wants to know how. The alchemical runoff poisoning them comes from the [[Aedir Signalpost]].
2. **[[Signal of Intent]] ([[Traversing the Tower]], [[Multifaceted]]).** The Aedir military research tower and the destroyed Shent machine are the oldest mechanisms Mig has ever seen. Mig starts a notebook of designs. What she learns from the Shent machine later helps finish Olli's core.
3. ◆ **[[Glitter in the Dark]] ([[Junkyard Cogs]], [[In League with Silver Beam]]).** [[Vartholomew Chess]], an Arcturian artificer, builds constructs carefully and with respect. The Silver Beam Consortium offers Mig funding, resources and a seat at their table. Does Mig side with the craftsman or the investors?
4. **[[Ancient Paths]] ([[Mythspire Observatory]], [[Impossible Skin]]).** The observatory's ancient mechanisms are a puzzle box for Mig. At the shrine, a smith's offering to Steelsong is still warm.
5. ◆ **[[Diplomatic Impunity]] ([[Foreign Influence]], Spellbreaker Tower [[Display Room]] and [[Surge Walkers]]).** The Tayan Ambassador [[Loris Tezran]] notices Mig's guns. In the Spellbreaker Tower's display room she finds early Tayan rifles, and she sees the Surge Walkers' cores up close. A Tayan contact offers an introduction to the Mechanica. Does she accept?
6. **[[Ember's Bounty]] (Ordain, custom scene).** The ship's secret gun deck of rune-etched ballistae that need no ammunition is exactly the kind of fire-and-mechanism design Mig dreams about.
7. **[[Ruby Grove]] ([[Kadra Zann]], Chapter 4).** The temple honours Zor-Thal, the Builder, a Shent god of craft whose statue was "designed by masters of magical artifice." Mig sees what Shent craft could do at its height. If Olli is with her, this is where she first understands how he was made.
8. **[[The Flood and the Flame]] ([[Blasted Books]], [[Summoning the Spark]], Chapter 4).** Mig meets Kess while they're both researching in a library: two fire mages who are each sure they're right. In the [[Tomb of the Unifiers]], Mig gets to study the ancient Sedyri artifacts that balanced fire and flood. Mig may be the one who notices that Kess is leaving out the Ripple of the Rivers, which balances the Spark, and does nothing about it.
9. ◆ **Climax: [[The Flood and the Flame]] ([[Burning Hearts]], [[Citadel of Fire]]).** At the [[Elemental Forge]], Mig is the natural person to forge **[[Cinderbreaker]]**, the weapon that frees the imprisoned Ignarch and cuts off one of Kess's sources of power. When Kess is defeated, the party has to choose between killing her to banish the Spark and attempting the hard ritual that summons the Ripple to balance it. Characters attuned to Ragen automatically sense that tearing the Spark out of Kess will kill her. Mig is the fire mage in the room, and her choice should carry the most weight.
10. **Resolution (custom).** Depending on her ending, Mig goes to the Krackos Enclave or to the Tayan workshops in [[Caren'ac]].

### Ending A: Forgehand of Steelsong
Mig chooses craft that keeps things in balance. She forges Cinderbreaker and argues for the harder path of summoning the Ripple and saving Kess, the way Steelsong would value a careful, patient piece of work over a quick fix. Afterwards Steelsong invites Mig to her anvil. Using what she learned from Shent machines, Mig helps finish Olli's core.

- **Attunement:** Akon (Discipline), 3 points
- **Patron's Gift: Ruenfoe-Steel Revolver.** Steelsong forges Mig a firearm from the same Shent metal as her hammer. It's a magic weapon that deals fire damage. Once per Rest, Mig can fire a **forgefire round**: one target takes an extra 4d8 fire damage, and if it's wearing metal armour or is a construct, it has disadvantage on attacks until the end of its next turn. Mig can also repair a construct or Hulg'run, which restores hit points equal to twice her level, once per Rest.
- **Lasting Mark:** Mig gets an apprentice's mark from the Krackos Enclave, and Steelsong's forges and workshops on the Plateau are open to her. If Kess survived, she becomes Mig's friend and rival, and the people of Tajar remember Mig as the one who saved her.

### Ending B: Prodigy of the Mechanica
Mig chooses progress, whatever it costs. Kess dies, the Spark is driven out, and Mig catches part of it in a device she built herself **(custom)**. Lorgi deposed Fiyara long ago, and he has wanted a sample of her fire ever since. He invites Mig to the Tayan workshops in [[Caren'ac]], where she learns the secrets of Aethyra. Her inventions become terrifyingly powerful, and they now belong partly to an empire.

- **Attunement:** Ragen (Directness), 3 points
- **Patron's Gift: Spark Cylinder.** A firing chamber fitted to Mig's gun and charged with a captured piece of the Spark of the Savanna. Once per Rest, Mig fires a 30-ft cone of superheated flame. Each creature in it makes a Dexterity save, taking 6d6 fire damage on a failure or half on a success. After firing, the cylinder overheats, and Mig takes 1d6 fire damage at the start of each of her next two turns unless she spends an action to vent it. Followers of Fiyara can sense the cylinder.
- **Lasting Mark:** Mig gains a contact in the Mechanica and Lorgi's personal interest, and Tayan agents will help her when it suits them. Steelsong's followers see her as a defector, and the Krackos Enclave is closed to her. The Flamewardens of the Caryx Savanna won't forget what happened to Kess.

## Shared threads

- **Silax and Bones are two sides of one story.** Silax was raised to strike first. Kesh died because he refused to. Their climaxes happen in the same scene, [[Cost of Living]], where the Coursers strike first exactly as Kesh's enemy did.
  - If Silax takes The Swift Arrow and Bones takes Kesh's Shield, they become a pair: Silax strikes, Bones shields.
  - If Silax takes The Open Hand and Bones takes Kesh's Way, Kesh's mercy lives on in both of them, and Thayloc and Raineka are brought together.
  - If they split, the gap between them is good for drama in later sessions.
- **The Lumek connect them.** Luna Karrowrath's company (at [[The Blockaded Bridge]] and [[A Conflagration of Lumé]]) knows Bones as the "coward medic," admires Silax's instincts and wants Kesh's ashes back. Their opinions give the two characters' choices an audience.
- **[[Burning Hearts]] tests the whole party.** Whether to kill Kess or save her is a strike-or-mercy decision for Silax and Bones, a craft-or-power decision for Mig, and an encounter with Abyssal corruption for Olli.
- **Mig and Olli meet at the forge.** Mig wants to take Olli apart and Olli wants to know what he is. Kadra Zann, a Shent temple to the Builder, is a shared scene for both. If both take Ending A, they finish his core together at Steelsong's forge. If Olli takes Eyes in the Dark, Steelsong asks Mig to keep an eye on him.
- **Sigil and Steelsong are old friends**, and Sigil can transport himself to the Krackos Enclave. Olli's patron and Mig's patron working together is how those two endings link up.
- **Ruby Grove has something for both Ffion and Olli.** Ffion has the Most August Society, the living forest and Nimaelle. Olli has the Shent scrying device at Kadra Zann. The quest's own final choice, whether to reject or serve the Silent Pack, is a good temptation to aim at whichever of them is leaning toward their darker ending.
- **Katu's family keeps turning up.** Raineka is Bones's patron, [[Sorak the Stormheart]] is Katu's angry champion in The Flood and the Flame, and Aythorn is friends with the family. The Isle of Storms is still a good shared final scene for Bones and Ffion.
- **Silax and Olli were both made for war.** Silax was raised to fight and Olli was built to. If Steelsong wants to build more sentinels, Silax has the strongest opinion in the party.
- **Hidden truths.** Mioroth knows what Olli is, Aythorn knows what Ffion is looking for, and Raineka knows how Kesh died. All three are withholding it until the right moment.
