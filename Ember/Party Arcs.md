# Party Arcs

> [!info] For the GM
> Each character has one Shard God patron and a personal arc, and each arc ends in one of two ways.
>
> **How to read the tables:**
> - The **Beat description** column says what needs to happen in the story, without tying it to a quest. If you swap in a different quest or a custom scene, keep the beat.
> - The **Suggested quest** column gives an existing quest and how the beat could show up there.
> - Beats run in story order. If the party skips a suggested quest, move the beat to whatever comes next.
> - Suggestions marked **(custom)** don't exist in the vault yet.
>
> **Markers:**
> - ◆ marks a choice point. Note which way the character leans each time, because those leanings decide which ending they reach.
> - ↺ marks a flashback scene.
> - **Echo** marks an optional beat that comes after the climax and replays the arc's central question.

> [!tip] Reward structure
> Every ending gives the same three kinds of reward:
>
> 1. **Attunement:** 3 [[Attunement]] Points toward the attunement that fits the ending's Ideal, awarded at the climax. You can also give 1 point at each ◆ beat where the character leaned that way, as the [[Attunement]] guide allows.
> 2. **Patron's Gift:** a signature item or blessing. Each one can be used once per Rest unless it says otherwise.
> 3. **Lasting Mark:** a permanent change to the character's place in the world, such as an ally, a title or an open door.

## Silax: the one who strikes first

**Patron:** [[Thayloc]]
**Ancestry:** Cor'ak

Silax's clan prized violence above everything and taught that you always strike first. It never came naturally to him. Travelling with Bones, who refuses to fight, Silax has become the one who strikes so that Bones never has to, and he has proved good at it. Thayloc's edict is "Swift is my arrow for rotting hearts." He was once a god of festivals, and after failing to act in time during the Evernight he became a god who never waits. He sees in Silax the champion his clan raised him to be. Silax has to decide whose way he will follow: his clan's and Thayloc's, or Bones's.

| Step | Beat description | Suggested quest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. The charm | Silax comes across a sign of who Thayloc *used* to be, a god of feasts and festivals, and keeps a token of it. Soon after, a sudden threat makes Silax's clan instincts take over before he can think. | **[[The Winding Trail]] (Dusktide Festival, [[Helkas]]):** An elderly vendor sells broken silver-arrow charms and remembers "the god of good feasts." When the Otherhood of Fortune attacks the festival, Silax reacts first. |
| 2. The mirror | Silax meets someone who fights for the joy of it and mocks him for hesitating. They show what Silax's clan expected him to become. | **[[Over The Moon]] ([[Lightning in a Bottle]]):** Rorhim Iron-Cask of the [[Fulgurite Blades]], a cheerful Cor'ak brawler, teases Silax for hesitating "like someone raised in a city." |
| 3. ◆ First fire | Silax faces undead for the first time, and Thayloc's silver fire appears in his strikes. He has to choose between striking first and holding back so Bones can reach the wounded. | **[[Crumbling Sanctuary]] ([[Harrowed Crossing]]):** [[Sodden Corpse]]s rise from the river while the party fords it. Silax's hits leave silver flame on them. |
| 4. ◆ The invitation | Thayloc's allies notice the fire, praise Silax's instincts, look down on Bones and invite Silax to join their war on the undead. | **[[Smoldering Cinders]] ([[A Conflagration of Lumé]]):** [[Luna Karrowrath]]'s Flameguard serve [[Lumé]], Thayloc's ally. They invite Silax on their crusade to the [[Barrows]]. |
| 5. ◆ Climax: who strikes first | Thayloc's own followers strike first against someone harmless. Silax has to choose between his clan's and Thayloc's way, and Bones's. This should be the same scene as Bones's climax. | **[[Unfinished Business]] ([[Cost of Living]]):** [[Thayloc Courser]]s ambush the party to seize [[Agraband Swift]], a peaceful [[Soulbound]]. |
| 6. ◆ Echo: the test | Silax is challenged to prove himself to warriors who respect strength. He can win by force or by talking. | **[[Proving Your Metal]] (Chapter 4, [[Redrak Fields]]):** A [[Burnished Hand]] patrol led by Carver Pela Melir. The event supports both persuasion and martial contests. |
| 7. Resolution | Silax meets Thayloc face to face, in the middle of the god's war, and acts on the choice he made at the climax. | **(custom) [[Mistwalk]] and [[Khar'koridax]] ([[Barrows]]):** The Flameguard's crusade gathers at Mistwalk. Thayloc arrives to clear out the Varún tomb, and Silax meets him at its entrance. |

### Ending A: The Swift Arrow
Silax accepts what his clan taught him and gives it a purpose by joining Thayloc's war on the undead. He stops apologising for striking first. Bones's way remains something Silax protects without sharing.

- **Attunement:** Ragen (Directness), 3 points
- **Patron's Gift: Heartbow Splinter.** The arrow charm hardens into silver. Silax has advantage on initiative rolls. When Silax hits an undead creature, it bursts into silver flame and takes an extra 2d8 radiant damage, and it can't regain hit points until the start of Silax's next turn.
- **Lasting Mark:** Silax becomes a recognised Courser of Thayloc. The Flameguard, the Coursers and the defenders of Mistwalk become allies. Sionia's followers, and any Soulbound the party meets, become wary of Silax. Silax's clan would be proud of him, which may not feel as good as he expected.

### Ending B: The Open Hand
Silax refuses to strike first. He protects the harmless, and when he meets Thayloc he talks to him rather than fighting beside him, offering the god the lesson Bones taught him. For the first time since the Evernight, Thayloc lays down the Heartbow for a night and holds a festival.

- **Attunement:** Heart (Vitalism), 3 points
- **Patron's Gift: Feastmaker's Arrow.** The charm turns warm gold. As an action, Silax declares a truce. Every creature within 30 ft that can hear Silax must succeed on a Wisdom save (DC 8 + proficiency + Constitution modifier) or be unable to make attacks until the end of Silax's next turn. Undead are immune. **If a creature attacks Silax or Bones during the truce, Silax can make one attack against it as a reaction.** During a Rest, the party regains extra hit points equal to Silax's level.
- **Lasting Mark:** Thayloc's cult starts to change and the Coursers spare harmless undead. Thayloc personally owes Silax a favour. Silax has broken with his clan, which a future custom scene could follow up.

## Bones: the coward medic

**Patron:** [[Raineka]]
**Ancestry:** Fej
**Told through:** present-day beats and ↺ flashbacks

Bones grew up on the Lumek front lines, where the Fej have fought the undead [[Moiran]] for generations under [[Lumé]]. He refused to carry a blade. The soldiers called him a coward and still lined up for his stitches. His commander, **Commander Kesh Durannor** (rename freely), was the one person who respected that choice. In the end Kesh followed Bones's example by refusing to strike first, and died for it. Fej burn to ash when they die, and Bones carries Kesh's ashes. The Lumek forge the ashes of their dead into holy weapons of [[Ambral Bronze]], and Bones has refused to hand Kesh over. As Kesh lay dying, a hooded woman in a cloak that shimmered like rain knelt beside him and did not save him. Bones has followed Raineka ever since, wanting to know why, and to find out whether Kesh's mercy was right.

| Step | Beat description | Suggested quest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. ↺ The satchel | Something in the present reminds Bones of healing on the battlefield. *Flashback:* his first muster, when he refused the blade he was handed and Kesh gave him a medic's satchel instead: "Then you'd better be fast." | **[[Ooze Control]] ([[Stone Life]]), happening now:** [[Tauric]] explains that copper-fed oozes can heal injuries. |
| 2. ↺ The slur | Soldiers from Bones's past recognise him, call him "ash-hand" and demand to know why Kesh's ashes were never returned. *Flashback:* the field hospital, where the soldiers who mocked him by day begged for him at night. | **[[Crumbling Sanctuary]] ([[The Blockaded Bridge]]):** [[Luna Karrowrath]]'s company of [[Lumek]] warriors is stuck at the blockade. |
| 3. ↺ The question | Bones's reputation splits the people who served with him: some scorn him and some show him the scars he stitched. The ashes come up again. *Flashback:* Kesh asks Bones, privately, how he can stand never fighting back. | **[[Smoldering Cinders]] ([[A Conflagration of Lumé]]):** The Flameguard's bonfire ceremony to bless their crusade. Someone says Kesh "should be fighting with us." |
| 4. The lead | Bones finds out where Raineka can be found in person. | **[[To Fall and Fall Again]] ([[Magic Incarnate]]):** On a research roll of 20+, a priest of [[Katu]] lets slip that Raineka secretly loves [[Ordain]] and has a favourite spot there. |
| 5. ◆ The confrontation | Bones finds Raineka in the middle of a crisis full of patients and asks her about Kesh. She doesn't answer yet. Bones has to choose between staying with the patients and going after the cause. | **[[Spreading Sickness]]:** Forge workers are falling ill and refugees are close to rioting. Raineka appears hooded at a crowded Healing House. |
| 6. ◆↺ Climax: Kesh's last stand | A present-day ambush plays out like the day Kesh died, and Bones has to decide whether to treat someone others call a threat. *Flashback:* Kesh orders his company to hold against an enemy who seems to be surrendering, and dies shielding Bones. This should be the same scene as Silax's climax. | **[[Unfinished Business]] ([[Cost of Living]]):** The Coursers ambush the party to seize the Soulbound [[Agraband Swift]]. Bones has to decide whether to treat him. |
| 7. Echo: mercy or ending | The party faces a choice between killing someone dangerous and attempting a risky rescue, and they look to Bones. | **[[The Flood and the Flame]] ([[Burning Hearts]], Chapter 4):** Whether to kill the corrupted Kess the Firemage or attempt the ritual that might save her. |
| 8. Resolution | Raineka tells Bones the truth about Kesh's death. Kesh asked her to tend the others first, and she honoured his wish. Bones decides what Kesh's ashes will become. | **(custom) Isle of Storms:** Raineka brings Bones to her family's home. If the party has met [[Sorak the Stormheart]] at [[Katino Shrine]], Bones can tell him that Katu's family hasn't abandoned everyone. |

### Ending A: Kesh's Way
Bones decides that Kesh's mercy was right even though it killed him. Bones has Kesh's ashes forged into Ambral Bronze, but as a protective charm of the kind the Lumek wear against undeath and disease, not as a weapon. Raineka is moved and takes Bones as her first true confidant in centuries. Bones stays a pacifist to the end.

- **Attunement:** Heart (Vitalism), 3 points
- **Patron's Gift: Raineka's Hood.** A blue cloak that shimmers like falling rain, fastened with an Ambral Bronze clasp made from Kesh's ashes. As a reaction when an ally within 30 ft drops to 0 hit points, Bones can let a gentle rain fall on them. The ally drops to 1 hit point instead and gains temporary hit points equal to Bones's level. While Bones wears the hood, hostile creatures have disadvantage on attacks against Bones on the first turn of combat if Bones hasn't attacked.
- **Lasting Mark:** Bones learns Raineka's hidden spot in Ordain and can find her there. Among the Lumek, "ash-hand" stops being an insult, and the clasp is the first Ambral Bronze in memory made for healing. Luna Karrowrath's company owes Bones a debt. Healing Houses across the Plateau take him in.

### Ending B: Kesh's Shield
Bones decides that Kesh died because nobody was protecting the person showing mercy. Bones still doesn't become a killer, but he stops letting the people he heals stand unprotected. He has Kesh's ashes forged into a shield-talisman so that Kesh can still protect people. Raineka respects the decision and keeps her distance.

- **Attunement:** Akon (Discipline), 3 points
- **Patron's Gift: Stormwall Charm.** An Ambral Bronze talisman forged from Kesh's ashes. As an action, Bones raises a 15-ft-radius ring of driving rain centred on him for 1 minute. Bones and allies inside have half cover against ranged attacks and advantage on death saves. Undead that start their turn inside take 2d6 radiant damage.
- **Lasting Mark:** The Lumek honour Bones as a battlefield healer and give him a place at Lumé's front if he ever wants it. Thayloc's Coursers also respect him.

## Olli: the sentinel who forgot

**Patron:** [[Sigil]]
**Also involved:** [[Mioroth]], [[Vhismara]], [[Steelsong]] (through Mig)
**Ancestry:** Hulg'run

Olli is a Shent sentinel, built before the Shent fell to watch for the Abyss, and woken because it is stirring again. Olli doesn't remember this and thinks his pull toward "the Shroud" is curiosity. Sigil has catalogued almost everything in the world, but his entry on Shent sentinels has its pages missing, and he wants Olli's story preserved before it's lost again. Shent ruins recognise Olli, Mioroth stays silent about him, and an Outer God offers him the thing he wants most, which is understanding. Olli has to choose between the purpose he was built for and the void that purpose was meant to watch.

> [!warning] Player knowledge
> The vault warns that player characters normally don't know the words Abyss, Outer Gods or Abyssal Shear. Olli should feel drawn to "the Shroud" without knowing why, and the real names come out during play.

The ruins wake | Shent work reacts to Olli, and another Hulg'run notices that something about Olli is unusually old. | **[[Ooze Control]] ([[Stone Life]], [[Yakoshta Mine]]), happening now:** The Shent runes in the ooze basin flare when Olli gets close. [[Tauric]] notices that Olli's spirit core feels older than any he's met. |
| 2. The one who knows | Someone who knows exactly what Olli is meets him and says nothing. ### Ending A: The Sentinel Restored
Olli turns down the Voice and takes back his purpose. Sigil's archive and Mioroth's memories restore his past. At Steelsong's forge, with Mig helping, the crystal at his centre is finished. Olli chooses to keep watching, this time because he wants to.

- **Attunement:** Signara (Curiosity), 3 points
- **Patron's Gift: Sentinel Core.** Olli always knows when Abyssal energy or an Abyssal creature is within 120 ft. Once per Rest, as a reaction when Olli or an ally within 30 ft fails a save against a charm, fear or psychic effect, Olli can make them reroll it and use the new result.
- **Lasting Mark:** Sigil writes Olli's story into the archive and gives him a key to Oldcraft Lodge. Shent ruins open to him. Mioroth treats Olli as an heir. If the Kadra Zann scrying device survives, Olli can learn to use it.

### Ending B: Eyes in the Dark
Olli listens to the Voice. He doesn't serve it. He learns from it and gets the understanding he wanted, at a price. He now sees the Abyss more clearly than any mortal alive, and it sees him back. Sigil seals Olli's entry in the archive, and doesn't say whether that's to protect Olli or to protect everyone else from him.

- **Attunement:** Abyss (Nihilism), 3 points. If the party gained a [[Beast Mark]], Olli carries the deepest one.
- **Patron's Gift: Void-Touched Core.** The crystal turns black with a green glow in its depths. Once per Rest, as an action, Olli opens a 10-ft-radius rift of void within 60 ft. Creatures inside must make a Wisdom save or take 3d8 psychic damage and be frightened until the end of their next turn. Olli can also understand any Abyssal speech or script.
- **Lasting Mark:** Olli can ask the Snarling Voice one question per chapter and always gets a true answer, but each question costs him a Beast Mark. [[Spectra]] takes a personal, wary interest in Olli. Steelsong asks Mig to keep an eye on him.

## Ffion: are plants people?

**Patron:** [[Aythorn]]
**Ancestry:** Thornling

Ffion is fashionable and wants to know whether other plants can think. Aythorn, the Plateau's beloved trickster god, claims to be an ascended Thornling but doesn't look or act like one. The truth is that Aythorn was once an ordinary plant that woke up. Following that question takes Ffion from crawling hedges to a fungus that speaks, then to a forest that fights back and a living tree from another realm, and finally to a creeping corruption of obsidian vines. Ffion has to decide what to do with the answer: protect every waking plant, or protect the garden from the ones that would consume it.

| Step | Beat description | Suggested quest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. ◆ First signs | Ffion finds a plant behaving as if it wants something. Ffion can try to communicate with it or just deal with it. | **[[Thorny Predicaments]] ([[Planting a Seed]]):** [[Edivel Sprout]]'s spell has animated a hedge that's crawling up the road. |
| 2. A living place | Ffion visits a place grown rather than built, and wonders whether the place itself is alive. | **[[Thorny Predicaments]] ([[Natural Wonders]], [[Bridging the Gap]]):** [[Brevin]], the seed-grown town, and its damaged bridge. |
| 3. The deflection | Ffion meets Aythorn and asks about their origin. Aythorn dodges with a joke and a gift. | **[[Thorny Predicaments]] ([[Aythorn's Blessings]]):** Aythorn gives Ffion a flower crown that turns toward Ffion every morning, and says, "Ask the flowers." |
| 4. ◆ A plant that speaks | Ffion meets plant life that is unmistakably aware and also dangerous. Ffion decides whether to talk to it or destroy it. | **[[Strange Thornlings]] (Standalone Event, Pathways):** The Sporix is a parasitic fungus that names itself and takes over Thornling hosts. |
| 5. The hint | At a glamorous social event, Aythorn lets slip a clue about their past. | **[[Disgraced House]] ([[The Marlstone Gala]]):** Aythorn is a guest who adores the flowers, and admits they were "a bit like those flowers once." |
| 6. Fashion with politics | Ffion's love of fashion runs into the politics of the industry behind it. *(Side beat)* | **[[Local Color]]:** The murals take aim at House Salva's grip on Ordain's textile and fashion industry. |
| 7. Kindred spirits | Ffion meets a group of Aythorn's devotees who share Ffion's style and flair, and they lead Ffion toward a place where plants are strange and powerful. | **[[Ruby Grove]] ([[Just a Taste]], Chapter 4):** The Most August Society of Aythorn, including Barka, a Thornling worshipper. They introduce Ruby and invite the party to [[Talei]]. |
| 8. ◆ The living forest | Ffion sees plants that clearly have wills of their own, some protective and some predatory. Ffion has to choose between siding with the forest and siding with the people it's swallowing. | **[[Ruby Grove]] ([[Local Experts]], [[Where Shadows Lie]]):** The writhing black aspens of the [[Bloodwoods]] ([[Traversing the Bloodwoods]]). The [[Arbore Sanctorus]], a living tree whose rooms are carved from its body. Ffion can ask its planter, [[Nimaelle]], whether the tree minds. The [[Shadebranch]] sap at [[Kadra Zann]] turns people into Grayling. |
| 9. ◆ Climax: the waking vines | A spreading, corrupted plant life is close to becoming aware. Ffion has to choose between trying to save it and cutting it out. | **[[Disturbed Earth]] (Chapter 4):** The obsidian vines spreading from [[Slaith]]'s corruption across the Redrak Fields. |
| 10. Resolution | Aythorn finally tells the whole truth, and how they say it depends on Ffion's choice. | **(custom) Aythorn's next party:** Aythorn could visit [[Brevin]] again, or throw a celebration in Talei with the Most August Society. |

### Ending A: The Voice of the Green
Ffion tries to save the vines and frees some of them from the corruption. Aythorn finally confesses the truth about where they came from and recognises Ffion as a fellow speaker for the green. Ffion becomes the first mortal advocate for plants that are waking up.

- **Attunement:** Orbis (Spontaneity), 3 points
- **Patron's Gift: Aythorn's Living Couture.** An outfit grown from living plants that blooms to match any style. Ffion can speak with plants at will and gets advantage on social checks at formal events. Once per Rest, Ffion can ask the outfit to animate nearby plants: a 20-ft square becomes difficult terrain, and one creature in it must make a Strength save or be restrained until the end of its next turn.
- **Lasting Mark:** Brevin and the Agrimage Circle treat Ffion as an authority on plant awareness. The Most August Society of Aythorn adopts Ffion as a guest of honour. Aythorn calls Ffion "Boba," a Thornling word for close family. Nimaelle and the Sanguinaries welcome Ffion deeper into the Arbore Sanctorus than most visitors get.

### Ending B: The Gardener's Shears
Ffion decides that awareness doesn't give something the right to consume others, which the Sporix and the Shadebranch both showed, and cuts out the corruption. Aythorn is sad and still tells the truth, but warns Ffion that "some of what you prune might have been like me."

- **Attunement:** Cora (Pragmatism), 3 points
- **Patron's Gift: Thornsilk Shears.** A pair of fashionable silver shears that can be used as a weapon. Attacks with them deal an extra 1d8 damage to plant creatures and creatures infected by parasites. Once per Rest, Ffion can cut a disease, curse or parasitic infection out of a willing creature, ending one such effect. This includes a Grayling transformation that hasn't finished.
- **Lasting Mark:** The Agrimage Circle and [[House Lilifeld]] honour Ffion for saving the harvest. Talei's ruby collectors trust Ffion. The Sporix, the Silent Pack and anything like them will recognise Ffion as an enemy.

## Mig: the gunsmith's question

**Patron:** [[Steelsong]]
**Rival influence:** [[Lorgi]]
**Ancestry:** Altyra

Mig is a fire mage and gunslinger who understands the world by taking it apart to see how it works. Her own liquid-metal body is one more mechanism she means to figure out. Steelsong, the goddess of craftsmanship, invention and fire, builds by hand at the Krackos Enclave forge to protect her people. Lorgi, the Wild God of fire and technology, leads the Tayan Empire's Mechanica, and his Aethyra breakthroughs are faster and more powerful but serve an empire. Firearms are Tayan technology, so Mig's craft already leads toward Lorgi. Eventually Mig meets her own reflection in a young fire mage who wanted only fire and was taken by [[Fiyara]], Lorgi's sister, who was corrupted by the Abyss. Mig has to decide what her fire, and her inventions, are for.

> [!note] Lorgi is a Wild God
> Wild Gods can't hear prayers from far away. Mig has to deal with Lorgi in person or through Tayan agents. That makes him a rival influence rather than a patron.

| Step | Beat description | Suggested quest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. How does it work? | Mig comes across a strange process she can't help taking apart, and a sign that someone's careless experiments are causing harm. | **[[Ooze Control]] ([[Stone Life]]), happening now:** Feeding metal dust to oozes changes them: iron makes them magnetic and silver makes them electric. The poisoning runoff comes from the [[Aedir Signalpost]]. |
| 2. Ancient machines | Mig studies machines far older and more advanced than anything made today, and starts a notebook of designs. What she learns will matter later, for Olli. | **[[Signal of Intent]] ([[Traversing the Tower]], [[Multifaceted]]):** The Aedir military research tower and the destroyed Shent machine in the Kaleidoscope Caverns. |
| 3. ◆ Craftsman or investors | Mig has to choose between a careful craftsman who respects what they make and wealthy backers who offer resources for results. | **[[Glitter in the Dark]] ([[Junkyard Cogs]], [[In League with Silver Beam]]):** [[Vartholomew Chess]] and the Chessmen, against the Silver Beam Consortium's funding and servitor conversions. |
| 4. Steelsong's trace | Mig comes across a sign of Steelsong's worship and an ancient puzzle of mechanisms. | **[[Ancient Paths]] ([[Mythspire Observatory]], [[Impossible Skin]]):** The observatory's old mechanisms, and a smith's offering to Steelsong that's still warm. |
| 5. ◆ The empire's offer | Agents of the Tayan Empire notice Mig's guns and offer her a way into Lorgi's Mechanica. | **[[Diplomatic Impunity]] ([[Foreign Influence]], Spellbreaker Tower [[Display Room]] and [[Surge Walkers]]):** Ambassador [[Loris Tezran]], the early Tayan rifles on display, and the Surge Walkers' cores. |

| 7. Craft at its peak | Mig sees what ancient divine craft could achieve. If Olli is with her, it's where she first understands how he was made. | **[[Ruby Grove]] ([[Kadra Zann]], Chapter 4):** The temple of Zor-Thal, the Builder, and his statue, "designed by masters of magical artifice." |
| 8. The reflection | Mig meets a fire mage who is just as sure of herself and is about to make a dangerous mistake. | 10. Resolution | Mig goes to the workshop she chose. | **(custom):** The Krackos Enclave, if she took Ending A, or the Tayan workshops in [[Caren'ac]], if she took Ending B. |

### Ending A: Forgehand of Steelsong
Mig chooses craft that keeps things in balance. She forges Cinderbreaker and argues for the harder path of summoning the Ripple and saving Kess, the way Steelsong would value a careful, patient piece of work over a quick fix. Afterwards Steelsong invites Mig to her anvil. Using what she learned from Shent machines, Mig helps finish Olli's core.

- **Attunement:** Akon (Discipline), 3 points
- **Patron's Gift: Ruenfoe-Steel Revolver.** Steelsong forges Mig a firearm from the same Shent metal as her hammer. It's a magic weapon that deals fire damage. Once per Rest, Mig can fire a **forgefire round**: one target takes an extra 4d8 fire damage, and if it's wearing metal armour or is a construct, it has disadvantage on attacks until the end of its next turn. Mig can also repair a construct or Hulg'run, which restores hit points equal to twice her level, once per Rest.
- **Lasting Mark:** Mig gets an apprentice's mark from the Krackos Enclave, and Steelsong's forges and workshops on the Plateau are open to her. If Kess survived, she becomes Mig's friend and rival, and the people of Tajar remember Mig as the one who saved her.

### Ending B: Prodigy of the Mechanica
Mig chooses progress, whatever it costs. Kess dies, the Spark is driven out, and Mig catches part of it in a device she built herself **(custom)**. Lorgi deposed Fiyara long ago, and he has wanted a sample of her fire ever since. He invites Mig to the Tayan workshops in [[Caren'ac]], where she learns the secrets of Aethyra. Her inventions become terrifyingly powerful, and they now belong partly to an empire.

- **Attunement:** Ragen (Directness), 3 points
- **Patron's Gift: Spark Cylinder.** A firing chamber fitted to Mig's gun and charged with a captured piece of the Spark of the Savanna. Once per Rest, Mig fires a 30-ft cone of superheated flame. Each creature in it makes a Dexterity save, taking 6d6 fire damage on a failure or half on a success. After firing, the cylinder overheats, and Mig takes 1d6 fire damage at the start of each of her next two turns unless she spends an action to vent it. Followers of Fiyara can sense the cylinder.
- **Lasting Mark:** Mig gains a contact in the Mechanica and Lorgi's personal interest, and Tayan agents will help her when it suits them. Steelsong's followers see her as a defector, and the Krackos Enclave is closed to her. The Flamewardens of the Caryx Savanna won't forget what happened to Kess.

## Shared threads

- **Silax and Bones are two sides of one story.** Silax was raised to strike first. Kesh died because he refused to. Their climaxes should be the same scene, and the suggested one is [[Cost of Living]], where the Coursers strike first exactly as Kesh's enemy did. If you move one climax, move the other with it.
  - If Silax takes The Swift Arrow and Bones takes Kesh's Shield, they become a pair: Silax strikes, Bones shields.
  - If Silax takes The Open Hand and Bones takes Kesh's Way, Kesh's mercy lives on in both of them, and Thayloc and Raineka are brought together.
  - If they split, the gap between them is good for drama in later sessions.
- **The Lumek connect them.** Luna Karrowrath's company knows Bones as the "coward medic," admires Silax's instincts and wants Kesh's ashes back. Their opinions give the two characters' choices an audience.
- **[[Burning Hearts]] tests the whole party.** Whether to kill Kess or save her is a strike-or-mercy decision for Silax and Bones, a craft-or-power decision for Mig, and an encounter with Abyssal corruption for Olli.
- **Mig and Olli meet at the forge.** Mig wants to take Olli apart and Olli wants to know what he is. Kadra Zann, a Shent temple to the Builder, is a shared scene for both. If both take Ending A, they finish his core together at Steelsong's forge. If Olli takes Eyes in the Dark, Steelsong asks Mig to keep an eye on him.
- **Sigil and Steelsong are old friends**, and Sigil can transport himself to the Krackos Enclave. Olli's patron and Mig's patron working together is how those two endings link up.
- **Ruby Grove has something for both Ffion and Olli.** Ffion has the Most August Society, the living forest and Nimaelle. Olli has the Shent scrying device at Kadra Zann. The quest's own final choice, whether to reject or serve the Silent Pack, is a good temptation to aim at whichever of them is leaning toward their darker ending.
- **Katu's family keeps turning up.** Raineka is Bones's patron, [[Sorak the Stormheart]] is Katu's angry champion in The Flood and the Flame, and Aythorn is friends with the family. The Isle of Storms is still a good shared final scene for Bones and Ffion.
- **Silax and Olli were both made for war.** Silax was raised to fight and Olli was built to. If Steelsong wants to build more sentinels, Silax has the strongest opinion in the party.
- **Hidden truths.** Mioroth knows what Olli is, Aythorn knows what Ffion is looking for, and Raineka knows how Kesh died. All three are withholding it until the right moment.
