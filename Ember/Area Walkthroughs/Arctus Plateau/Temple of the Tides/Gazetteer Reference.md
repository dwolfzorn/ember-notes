# Gazetteer Reference

> [!quote] Read Aloud
> As you move down the sloping lakeshore, an ancient ruin constructed of a Sedyri temple comes into view, deep below the surging waters. Despite the ravages of water and time, the Cinderspar walls stand vigil on the lake bottom, as the lake's gentle currents stream in and out of the structure's grates and vents.

This ancient temple constructed by the last remnants of the Sedyri on the Arctus Plateau honors one of the Savanna's two great elementals forces: the water that both nourishes the Savanna's life, and threatens to drown it as Mayis crosses the sky above. The temple's chambers are designed to accept the very waters it venerates, with complex mechanisms for controlling currents and redirecting water flows.

### Environment

Like the Sedyri themselves, the construction of the Temple of the Tides embraces the same contradictions as the Caryx Savanna in which it lay. During the Savanna's fire season, with Ragen high in the sky, the temple sits exposed to the air — once, an ancient bridge crossed the ravine which otherwise prevented access during Ragen's reign. Oppositely, as Mayis rose overhead, the lake waters rose, and the temple itself was submerged. Sedyri worshippers, enchanted with ancient magic permitting them to breathe the waters, dove into the lake and swam their way through the temple's narrow halls.

### Ecology

Around the temple grows unique flora, which long ago adapted to spending months at a time underwater, as well as equally long stretches exposed to the open air. Where once dutiful Sedyri temple-keepers kept such plants from creeping into the temple's halls, their leaves now sprout out of cracks in the temple's edifice. When the temple lies submerged, the occasional fish and other sea creatures swim its currents and dart in and out of its passageways.

### Lore

The Temple of the Tides was an artifact of the Sedyri culture's final days on the Arctus Plateau, just before they melted away into the Plateau's other cultures. In its construction, the last of the Arcturian Sedyri experimented with venerating opposites at a distance — though the accompanying fire temple has long been lost to time, and is believed destroyed. This separation presaged the gradual dissolution of Sedyri culture on the Plateau.

However, the temple's most active days, it was an awesome sight to behold … for those skilled enough to do behold it. During the Savanna's flood season, the Temple's entrance halls served as challenges to young Sedyri initiates of both Unifiers and temple-keepers alike. With little to no guidance from their forebears, such young dedicants were expected to solve the puzzle of the temple's obstructing tides, relying on the power of their wits — and their magic to keep themselves breathing throughout. Those who passed could participate in worship services on the temple's upper level, or observe its mechanisms on the lower level.

In the modern era, the temple sits mostly above the lake waters. However, those who visit during the events of [[The Flood and the Flame]] will find the temple fully submerged once again — the waters lifted by the angry water elementals and disturbed temple guardians, woken from their peaceful slumber by the disturbances to the Savanna's seasonal cycles.
