# Brimtown

> [!warning] Gamemaster
> #### Gamemaster's Summary
>
> This location exists at ⬢ s.3227.2880 in the [[Golden Flats]].

Brimtown serves as a dusty, salt-of-the-earth threshold to the grander city below, perpetually blanketed by a thin layer of dust blown in from the neighboring Golden Flats and from the caravans that roll through on their way to Ordain. Despite its official status as a district of Arcturel, its identity is somewhat separate, defined by the constant clatter of carriage wheels, the braying of draft beasts, and the weary sighs of travelers or workers from Arcturel who see it as a necessary checkpoint or a grim place of work rather than a home.

## Landmarks

Only a few hardy citizens make Brimtown their home, maintaining some of the barns, storage sheds, and barns that define the outpost district on the edge of Arcturel. Many workers live within Arcturel nearby instead, choosing to commute to Brimtown and earn a living helping caravaners, loading goods and tending to beasts of burden.

**The Emberstone Beacon Watchtower**

This lone watchtower is built into the remains of a desiccated tree at the sinkhole's edge. Home to a small family known as the Brimtown Watchers, they keep a keen eye on Arcturel's surroundings and help caravans find their way to the city after dark.

**The Edge of CivilizationWaystation**

A ramshackle waystation near the sinkhole’s edge that hosts a tavern alongside an assortment of stables. In recent years, The Edge has desperately tried to shed its grim interior with a makeover and market itself to higher-paying customers, but it has yet to achieve lasting success. It is owned and operated by a congenial woman named Onsi Urganzi.

## Life in Brimtown

Life in Brimtown is a rhythmic cycle of arrivals and departures, where the air is permanently thick with the smell of beasts of burden, dry hay, and a faint hint of odd gases rising from the nearby sinkhole. Residents live in a state of perpetual transience, their daily routines dictated entirely by the schedules of incoming trade caravans and the weather blowing off the Flats. It is a noisy, hard-scrabble existence where a quiet night is a luxury, and nearly everyone is either selling something to a traveler or saving up enough coin to afford a better life elsewhere.

### Culture

The culture of the district is fiercely pragmatic and somewhat detached, as the few local families have grown accustomed to making fast acquaintances but few lasting friendships due to the rotating population. There is a deeply ingrained superstition about the sinkhole's edge and the monsters of the flats, leading to a communal ritual in which a copper coin is tossed over the edge for good luck before anyone sets out on a long journey.

### Defense

Brimtown relies almost entirely on its single watchtower to spot encroaching creatures, megafauna, or bandits before they can catch the settlement unawares. In the event of a genuine monster attack, the town's emergency strategy is not to stand and fight, but to retreat across the nearby bridge that connects to Arcturel proper to the east.

### Economy

The local economy is purely service-driven, which absorbs the coin of traveling merchants before they ever set foot in the city proper. Substantial wealth is generated through steep stabling fees, secure cargo storage, and the sale of basic provisions like rations, fresh water, and beast fodder. Because the road connects directly to Ordain, Brimtown also enjoys a lucrative monopoly on the initial wear-and-tear repairs of caravan wagons, keeping a steady rotation of blacksmiths and wheelwrights employed.

### Politics

Brimtown has no direct governance, effectively leaving the day-to-day to an informal coalition of stable masters and tavern owners. This neglect by some of the richer businesses and the House Cevher breeds a quiet, simmering resentment among the residents, who resent paying taxes to Arcturel proper, which barely acknowledges their existence unless trade is disrupted.

### Lore

Brimtown was originally established as a chaotic, temporary camp of tents and lean-tos for the miners and abuilders who first discovered the great sinkhole centuries ago. As Arcturel grew downward into the earth and became a proper subterranean town and city, the surface camp refused to die, slowly solidifying into a permanent settlement of wood and stone as enterprising locals realized travelers would always need a place to rest before descending or moving on to Ordain.
