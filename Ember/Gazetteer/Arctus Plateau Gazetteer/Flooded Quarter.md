# Flooded Quarter

> [!warning] Gamemaster
> #### Gamemaster's Summary
>
> This district exists at ⬢ s.3247.2892. It is located in the southmost hex of the city of Tajar, which itself lies along the southern coast of the [[Caryx Savanna]].
>
> - This district is indirectly featured in [[The Flood and the Flame]] Side Quest.

> [!info] Social
> #### At a Glance
>
> - **District Type:** Commercial (Maritime)
> - **Size:** 100-250 (Primarily commuting laborers and tourists)
> - **Services:** Docks, fishing, boat and foot tours of pre-Great Burning Tajar.
> - **Architecture**: Collapsed (Buildings burned then later flooded)

### Culture

Since the Great Burning, the Flood Quarter has become first and foremost a place of labor. Fisherman sail its waters, longshoreman unload cargo and march it across the soggy and uneven landscape, and even tour guides get their hands dirty maintaining walkable paths. There is an atmosphere of pride among the small number of Tajarins who still work in the Flooded Quarter — a shared sense that by continuing to make use of the collapsed district, they preserve the memory of the Flooded Quarter, and honor its history and people.

### Landmarks

**The Eternal Beacon**

Constructed of Cinderspar by the ancient Sedyri, this tall tower at the southern tip of the Flooded Quarter is one of the few structures to have survived the Great Burning intact. Before the Burning, the beacon at the tower's top was lit only at night, to guide ships in from the sea. Now, Flamekeepers Mara Tyr and Valira Pas keep constant vigil to maintain the flame through day and night — an ever-burning memorial to those lost when the Flooded Quarter burned.

**The Old Docks**

In the time since the Great Burning, the people of Tajar have built new docks on the seaward-side of the Communal Quarter — closer to actively inhabited areas. However, the narrow sea pass between the Communal Quarter and Historic Quarter can be difficult for larger ships to navigate. For this reason, engineer Palla Cern made it her personal project to restore the docks in the Flooded Quarter, even though those unloading cargo are in for quite a hike.

**The Fireline**

When journeying downslope from the Communal Quarter to the Flooded Quarter, the Fireline is an unmistakable landmark. Before it are the Communal Quarter's many buildings, after it are collapsed ruins, and in the middle, a wide no man's land. It was here that countless Flamewardens gave their lives, casting spells, retrieving wounded, and even resorting to simple thrown buckets of water in a desperate collective effort to turn back the raging fire. In the time since, the line has been left to nature, in honor of the Flamewardens who fought and died to hold it.

**Flamewardens' Training Grounds**

The few buildings in the Flooded Quarter sturdy enough to have survived the Great Burning without completely collapsing — and up-slope enough to have survived the seawater — are now used by the Flamewardens, both to train their initiates, and to experiment with new techniques for fighting off the Savanna's fires.

### Locals

**Reniss Yalira (Neutral Good, Arcturian Kiska, she/her)**

Renniss Yalira is the head of the Flooded Fishery, a collective fishery formed after the people and infrastructure which comprised the Tajar's older fishing initiatives were decimated by the lost in the Great Burning. This nimble Kiska is not just capable administrator, but also an incredibly skilled fisherman in her own right. She is one of the few people to have caught the rare Coriolis fish — or at least, to have *verifiably* done so.

**Palla Cern (Lawful Good, Arcturian Ashka, she/her)**

Broad-shouldered, barrel-chested, and rippling with muscle, Tajar's preeminent civil engineer is not afraid to get her hands dirty building the infrastructure she envisions. Given the frequency with which both fire and flood damage Tajar's infrastructure, Palla's role is a vital one, and she can be found overseeing construction across all three quarters. However, as often as possible, she steals away to the Flooded Quarter, working — sometimes entirely alone — on her personal pet project of restoring the Flooded Quarter's buildings, in the hopes of one day reinhabiting it.

**Flamekeepers Mara Tyr and Valira Pas (Lawful Good, Arcturian Ashka, she/her)**

In the wake of the Great Burning, the Flamewardens established a new rank, held only by Mara Tyr and Valira Pas: Flamekeeper. The only two Flamewardens to specialize in magic that *creates* fire, these two veteran spellcasters were assigned to the newly-designated Eternal Beacon. They live within its walls, working in shifts to ensure the light atop the coastal tower never goes out — rain or shine, day or night — in honor of those lost during that great and terrible fire.

**Kyr Penne (Neutral Good, Arcturian Ashka, they/them)**

Kyr Penne is the preeminent figure of the budding tourism industry in Tajar. Recent increases in trade with Ordain have brought a previously unheard of volume of travelers into the city, and Kyr — once little more than a thrill-seeking youth with a penchant for exploring the Flooded Quarter's ruins — took it upon herself to offer tours of this collapsed section of the city. The tours begin by boat, then proceed inland, and (for the more capable traveler) can even involve journeying inside partially collapsed buildings!

### Lore

Before the Great Burning, the Flooded Quarter was the economic center of Tajar. Tajar's docks and fisheries were incredibly lucrative, and brought significant resources into the city — resources that were shared collectively among Tajar's people. The destruction of this infrastructure introduced significant new scarcity, and presaged the shift of Tajar's economy from fishing (in the Flooded Quarter) to farming (in the Communal Quarter) and an accompanying shift in power from fishery operators to the Tajar Farming Cooperative. Some have seen this as a shift away from traditional Tajarin culture — especially under the leadership of Augus Karn, the Tajar Farming Cooperative administrator currently courting trade with Ordain — and the likes of Reniss Yalira and her fellow fisherman see their project as not just one of economic prosperity, but also preservation of traditional Tajarin ways of life.
