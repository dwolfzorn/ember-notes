### Environment

The strong winds that blow through the shrine at every hour of every day cannot possibly be natural — interpreted by the Acolytes of the Tempest as evidence that their oft-missing god Katu appreciates their worship, even in his absence. So too is the shrine beset by the Savanna's omnipresent hazards: wildfires, floods, lightning, and more. Worshippers of Katu, however, are no strangers to bad weather, and visit constantly to repair, maintain, and safeguard the shrine.

### Ecology

Though grasses attempt to encroach upon the edifice, diligent maintenance by the Acolytes of the Tempest keeps any unwelcome flora from climbing the shrine's steps. Animals, on the other hand, are welcome to ascend the shrine's platform, provided they do not make a home there that would exclude others visitors. Followers of Katu, after all, would never dare tell another living creature what it is or is not allowed to do with its own life.

### Lore

The Acolytes of the Tempest who constructed the shrine centuries ago chose its location for numerous overlapping reasons. Its presence along the coastline is a natural choice — the stormy seas are home to countless worshippers who petition Katu for fair weather. Less obvious to the uninitiated is the shrine's distance from Tajar; the city's traditional atmosphere and insistence on collective decision making chafes the highly individualist storm worshippers immensely. Known not even to the builder's themselves, the location is also resonant with pure elemental essence of water, due to the submerged Sedyri temple at the bottom of the nearby lake — a resonance that likely influenced the original Acolytes at a purely subconscious level.
